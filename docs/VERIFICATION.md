# Verifying the duration claim

> One container at 1 MW gives **3.56 h** at commissioning, **3.35 h** after a year and
> **2.30 h** in year twenty.

Generated by `npm run verify` from the same engine that makes the claim.

## That sentence is not one claim

It is six numbers multiplied together, and each is settled by a different document produced
by a different party. Nobody should be verifying the arithmetic — it is one line and it is
in `src/sizing/engine.ts` under test. **The inputs are the entire argument.**

```
  hours at 1 MW  =  nameplate × SOC window × depth of discharge × discharge path × retention
                    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── 
                                        rated power
                    less auxiliaries
```

With this container: **5,015.962 kWh** × **95.00%** × **90.00%** × **94.57%** × retention, less **500 kWh** a cycle, at **1 MW**.

| At | Retention | Deliverable | Hours at 1 MW |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Commissioning | 100.00% | 3.56 MWh | 3.56 h |
| End of year 1 | 95.00% | 3.35 MWh | 3.35 h |
| End of year 10 | 80.00% | 2.74 MWh | 2.74 h |
| End of year 20 | 69.00% | 2.3 MWh | 2.30 h |

## The six factors, and who settles each

| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Nameplate energy | 5,015.962 kWh | Computed: 4,992 cells × 3.2 V × 314 Ah. Cell marked `supplied`. | The cell maker, then the integrator | Cell data sheet (nominal voltage, rated capacity and the rate it is rated at) **and** the container bill of materials, so the cell count can be multiplied out. Capacity per **IEC 62620**. |
| 2 | Usable state-of-charge window | 95.00% | Platform default, editable per project. | The BMS configuration, which is the integrator | The BMS parameter sheet, or the supplier's written statement of *usable* energy against *nameplate*. This is a setting, not a property — it can be read off a commissioned system. |
| 3 | Depth of discharge | 90.00% | An operating choice carried from the application preset. | Whoever writes the contract | Nothing. It is a decision, and it belongs in the performance annexure beside the warranty it interacts with. |
| 4 | Discharge path efficiency | 94.57% | DC cable 0.25% + converter 1.50% + AC cable 0.25% + transformer 1.00%, all platform defaults. | The converter and transformer makers, plus the cable design | Converter efficiency curve measured per **IEC 61683** (weighted figures usually quoted per **EN 50530**, EU or CEC weighting) · transformer routine test report per **IEC 60076-1**, no-load and load loss · the cable sizing calculation for the run actually installed. |

The same six are on the **Design** tab of any project in the studio, under *Where the
nameplate goes*: one bar per factor, shrinking as each takes its share, with **Who settles
this** and **Evidence to ask for** on every step. They are generated from the same module as
this table, so the pack a buyer holds and the screen an engineer works on cannot drift apart.

## Then stop verifying factors and measure the result

Five of the six can be checked on paper before anything is bought. The sixth cannot: nobody
has a twenty-year measurement of a cell that has been in production for three years, and any
supplier who offers one is extrapolating. **Retention is not verified, it is warranted** — so
the thing to negotiate is the guarantee and its remedy, not the curve.

For everything else the authority is a measurement of the assembled system, not a
recalculation of its parts:

| Test | Standard | What it settles | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rated energy and rated power at the system boundary | **IEC 62933-2-1** — *Electrical energy storage (EES) systems, Part 2-1: unit parameters and testing methods* | Factors 1–5 together, as one measured number. A discharge at the declared rate from the declared upper limit to the declared lower one. | Factory acceptance, then site acceptance |
| Round-trip efficiency | **IEC 62933-2-1**, or the DOE-OE protocol **PNNL-22010 Rev 2 / SAND2016-3078** for duty-cycle-based figures | Factor 4 and factor 5 together, at the boundary you agreed | Site acceptance |
| Capacity test, repeated annually | The same procedure as at handover, written into the contract | Factor 6, as it actually happens | Every year of the warranty |

## Tolerances, which are generous unless you tighten them

A verified figure is a figure plus the band it was allowed to miss by, and the default bands
in the standards are wider than most buyers assume. Two worth naming in the contract rather
than inheriting:

- **Transformer losses.** IEC 60076 conventionally allows the measured loss to exceed the
  guaranteed value by up to **+15%** unless the contract says otherwise. On the transformer in
  this design that is the difference between a stated figure and one that is materially worse,
  every hour of every year. State the guaranteed no-load and load loss *and* the tolerance.
- **Capacity.** The measurement is only as good as its conditions. A capacity test is
  meaningless unless the rate, the ambient, the state-of-charge limits and the rest period
  before it are all fixed in the same document as the number.

Write the guaranteed value, the test that measures it, the conditions it is measured under and
the tolerance, together, in one annexure. Any one of the four missing makes the other three
unenforceable.

## The thing that makes two honest parties disagree

**The measurement boundary.** Every figure above changes depending on where it is measured
and what is inside the fence:

- **DC terminals or AC terminals?** The converter is worth several points.
- **Before or after the transformer?** Another point, and it is the customer's point.
- **Auxiliaries inside or outside?** On a liquid-cooled container in a hot climate this is the
  largest single term after the cells, and it is the one most often quietly excluded.
- **At what rate, and at what ambient?** Capacity measured at 0.2 C and 25 °C is not the
  capacity available at 0.5 C and 45 °C.

Agree the boundary in writing before anybody tests anything. Two parties measuring the same
container at different boundaries will both be right and will not agree, and that argument is
more expensive than the test.
