## The question "1 MW / 5 MWh" does not settle

Five megawatt-hours **delivered to the load** and five megawatt-hours **of nameplate** are different plants, and the gap is about a factor of two. Both are priced below, and which one is meant is the first thing to settle before anybody quotes.

| Reading | Contracted at the load | Nameplate installed | Duration at 1 MW | Containers | Conversion | Order value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 5 MWh delivered to the load | 5 MWh | 10 MWh | 5.00 h | 2 × SWESLC1331.2V314Ah | 1 × 1725 kW | ₹9.74 cr |
| one 5 MWh container, held for twenty years | 2.29 MWh | 5.02 MWh | 2.29 h | 1 × SWESLC1331.2V314Ah | 1 × 1725 kW | ₹5.26 cr |

A 5 MWh container is 5 MWh of **nameplate**. Between that and the meter sit the usable state-of-charge window, the depth of discharge, the conversion path, the auxiliaries and the capacity the cells will have lost by the end of the year the plant is sized for. One container carries 1 MW for **2.29 h** across twenty years with nothing added, not five. Five hours at the load takes two of them.

What one container gives at 1 MW, as the cells age:

| | At commissioning | End of year 1 | End of year 10 | End of year 20 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Deliverable | 3.56 MWh | 3.35 MWh | 2.74 MWh | 2.3 MWh |
| Hours at 1 MW | 3.56 h | 3.35 h | 2.74 h | 2.30 h |

A contract is written against the last of those, not the first. Sizing a single container to its year-one figure leaves it needing a **second whole container** the moment it fades — doubling the plant to hold a duty it was never quite able to carry.

## Reading A — 5 MWh delivered to the load

### The plant

- **2 × SWESLC1331.2V314Ah · 5.02 MWh container = 12 strings × 418 kWh (1331.2 V, 314 Ah each)**
- 1 × PCS 1725 kW — 1.73 MW of conversion against 1 MW contracted
- 1 × IDT 1600 kVA, 0.4/11 kV
- 9,984 × LFP 314 Ah prismatic in 96 packs, 24 parallel strings, 1040–1518.4 V DC
- 10 MWh nameplate · 7.11 MWh deliverable on day one (70.9% of nameplate)
- 0.100 C discharge, 0.100 C charge, against 0.50 C the pack sustains
- 87.2% round trip at the connection · 5.74 h to recharge at rated power
- 53 m² of enclosure footprint, 84 t

### How the fleet was arrived at

**Energy**

| Step | Figure | Why |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Contracted energy, at the load | **5 MWh** | 1 MW for 5 h, as asked for. |
| At the battery terminals | **5.29 MWh** | Divided by the 94.6% discharge path — DC cable, converter, AC cable and transformer. |
| Nominal capacity, new | **6.18 MWh** | Divided by 90.0% depth of discharge inside a 95.0% usable window: 85.5% of nameplate is available in a cycle. |
| Nominal capacity to install | **6.51 MWh** | Divided by 95.0% retention at year 1. Later years are met by augmentation rather than by buying them now. |
| Delivered by one enclosure | **3.35 MWh** | 3.85 MWh out of the enclosure, less 500 kWh a cycle for cooling, controls and communications. |
| Enclosures for the energy | **2** | 5 MWh contracted at 3.35 MWh delivered per enclosure, rounded up. |

**Power**

| Step | Figure | Why |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Contracted power, at the connection | **1 MW** | What the plant discharges at. |
| Charge power the window asks for | **999 kW** | 5 MWh back in over 5.74 h, grossed up for the 87.2% round trip. At rated power it would take 5.74 h. |
| Power the plant is sized on | **1 MW** | Discharging is the harder of the two. |
| Enclosures for the power | **1** | 1 MW at 2507.5 kW per enclosure, rounded up. |

- Energy sets the fleet: 2 enclosures against 1 for the power alone.
- The duty needs 1.49 enclosures and they are sold whole, so 2 are installed — 34% more than the duty strictly asks for.
- Conversion rounds up as well: 1 × 1725 kW is 1.73 MW installed against 1 MW asked for. It is the smallest converter in the catalogue that suits this string voltage.
